Privacy

Taskr keeps what a household types into it on a server, so that every member's phone shows the same split. This page says what that is, who can read it, which other companies handle it, and how to get it deleted. Taskr publishes no other privacy policy, so this is the one that governs.

What it stores

Everything in the table is kept on Taskr's server, in the household's own records. The last column says who in the household can read each row through the app. Every member can read every other member's email address; it is stated here plainly because a household app is likely to include people who did not expect that.

Everything you type has a limit. Taskr's server enforces these: a person's name is at most forty characters, a household's name sixty, a chore's name eighty, and a description you ask Taskr to work out two thousand, and that one is not kept. The app enforces these: a shopping list's or item's name is at most eighty characters, and an item's note a hundred and twenty. There is no other free-text field that is kept.

What it keeps on your device

In your browser's own storage, and nowhere else: your sign-in session, kept by the browser if Trust this device — stay signed in is ticked, or by this tab alone until the browser closes if it is not, with a marker saying so; which household you last switched to, so the app reopens on it; an invitation code and the name you typed with it before you were signed in, cleared the moment it is used or refused, and on sign-out; and whether you said Not now to installing Taskr (forgotten after thirty days) or installed it. While a calendar connection is in progress, the tab holds a one-off token and which household it is for, and forgets both on the way back or when the tab closes. The browser also keeps the app's own files so that it opens quickly; they hold nothing of yours.

Signing out removes all of it except the note about installing. Clearing the site's data in your browser removes everything.

Who else can read it

Every read the app makes goes through the database's own row-level rules, keyed on which household you are a member of. A member of one household cannot read another household's records, and the app has no way to ask for them.

As the person who runs Taskr I can read the database it stores this in. I do that only to fix a problem you have reported or to delete data you have asked me to delete. Stated 15 October 2026.

How to get it deleted

All four routes are in the app, on the Who tab. Deleting your account is also on the screen you see when you are signed in to no household.

Leave a household
Leave this household, near the foot of the Who tab. The chores dealt to you go to the others; any placed on you by hand become unassigned, and chores you finished stay finished without your name. Then your row and what hangs from it go: your minutes, which chores you were marked as unable to do, your calendar connection, and your record in the table above. Things you added to shopping lists, invitations you sent and calendar events you imported stay, without your name, and the record of which chores changed hands keeps an unnamed entry for you. If you had connected Google Calendar, Taskr asks Google to cancel its access before your key is deleted, unless another Taskr connection uses the same Google account, or the connection dates from before 17 September 2026 and Taskr does not know which account it was. If this was the only household you were in, your sign-in is deleted as well. An organizer cannot leave without first handing the household to another member who has signed in, or deleting it.
Delete a household
The organizer's Delete this household, near the bottom of the Who tab. It disappears from everyone's app at once. For seven days the organizer sees Restore with the household's name on signing in, and can bring it back whole. After that it is purged for good: Taskr asks Google to cancel each member's calendar access, unless another household uses the same Google account (or, for a connection made before 17 September 2026, the account is unknown), and every sign-in that belonged only to this household is deleted.
Be removed by the organizer
Your row and everything hanging from it go the same way. Taskr does not, in this case, tell Google, so if you had connected a calendar, cancel Taskr's access yourself from your Google account's connected apps.
Delete your account
Delete my account. On the Who tab it takes you through leaving, and leaving the last household you are in deletes your sign-in at once. If you are signed in but in no household, the screen you see has Delete my account, which deletes the sign-in immediately and cannot be undone; if you were in a household waiting to be deleted, your calendar connection there is cancelled first.

Nothing else is deleted on a timer. Apart from the seven-day purge of a deleted household, Taskr keeps what it stores until somebody deletes it by one of the routes above.

Children

Members of a household may be children; a child with sixty available minutes and a parent with three hundred is the case Taskr was built for. There is no age check. The organizer decides who is added, and since 11 September 2026 every new member is added by their own email address and sets their own password. Members added before that date with a PIN keep it.

Whether a child's own school calendar should be connected is a question Taskr has not settled, so nothing here recommends it.

Security

Passwords are held by the sign-in service, never by Taskr. The key Google issues for a connected calendar can be read only by Taskr's own server-side functions, and the credential those functions use never reaches the app in your browser; a check at build time refuses a build that would ship it. The row-level rules above are enforced by the database itself, not by the app asking nicely.
